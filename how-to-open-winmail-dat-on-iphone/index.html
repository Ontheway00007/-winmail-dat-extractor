How to open winmail.dat on iPhone and iPad

Apple Mail cannot unpack Outlook TNEF containers, so a winmail.dat attachment looks like a dead end on iOS. It is not. Save the file to Files, run it through a browser extractor and your documents come back with their original names.

By the Winmail.dat Extractor team·6 min read

What you need
  • An iPhone or iPad running any recent version of iOS or iPadOS.
  • The email containing winmail.dat, win.dat or an attachment shown as “Part 1.2”.
  • Safari or Chrome — no App Store download and no Outlook subscription.

Why iPhone shows winmail.dat instead of your file

Apple Mail follows internet email standards and does not implement Outlook’s proprietary TNEF encapsulation. When a message arrives with an application/ms-tnef part, iOS has nothing to open it with, so it presents the raw container. Tapping it shows a preview that fails, or an app-chooser sheet with no useful option.

No iOS setting changes this, and switching from Mail to Gmail or Outlook for iOS does not reliably help either — Outlook for iOS resolves some containers but not all. Extracting the container yourself is the dependable route.

The five-step walkthrough

1. Tap the attachment in your mail app

In Mail, scroll to the bottom of the message and tap the attachment once. Do not use a long press followed by Quick Look; the share sheet is what you need.

2. Save to Files

Tap the share icon (a square with an upward arrow), then Save to Files. Choosing On My iPhone › Downloads makes the file easy to find again. In Gmail for iOS, use the download arrow first, then share the downloaded item to Files.

3. Open the extractor

Open the extractor in Safari and tap Choose file. In the sheet that appears, select Choose File rather than Photo Library.

4. Select the container

Browse to the folder you used in step 2. If you cannot see the file, tap Browse at the bottom, then Recents — iOS lists recently saved documents there. Select it, and the extractor immediately reports how many attachments it recovered.

5. Download and unzip

Tap Download ZIP. Safari saves it to your Downloads location. Open Files, tap the ZIP once and iOS creates a folder with the same name containing your documents.

Tip: if the ZIP holds a single PDF, tap it and use the share icon to send it straight to Books, Drive or a colleague without extra steps.

Troubleshooting on iOS

SymptomWhat to do
The file picker will not show the containerUse Browse › Recents, or move the file into On My iPhone › Downloads and try again.
Attachment is greyed out in MailPull to refresh the mailbox so Mail finishes downloading the full message, then tap again.
Extraction reports no attachmentsThe container holds only formatting or a meeting request. Ask the sender to resend the files with HTML format.
ZIP will not openOpen it from the Files app rather than from the browser download list; Safari’s preview cannot unzip archives.
Recovered file has an odd nameRename it in Files and keep the extension, for example .pdf or .docx, so the right app opens it.

Notes for iPad

The steps are identical on iPadOS, with two conveniences: you can drag the attachment from Mail directly into the Files app, and you can drag the saved file from Files onto the extractor’s drop area in Safari using Split View. Everything else, including the ZIP handling, behaves the same way.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a winmail.dat app for iPhone?

Several exist, but you do not need one. A browser extractor does the same work without installing software or granting an app access to your files.

Can I open winmail.dat in Outlook for iOS?

Sometimes. Outlook for iOS resolves many TNEF containers server-side, so it is worth trying if you have the app, but it does not handle every case.

Where does Safari save downloaded files on iPhone?

By default to the Downloads folder in Files, either on the device or in iCloud Drive depending on your Safari settings under Settings, Apps, Safari, Downloads.

Why does my attachment appear as Part 1.2 on iPhone?

Mail assigns a generic name when the TNEF part has no filename. It is still the same container and can be saved and extracted in exactly the same way.

Does extracting on iPhone use my mobile data?

Only downloading the email attachment uses data. The extraction itself runs offline on the device, so no upload consumes your allowance.

Can I forward the recovered files from my iPhone?

Yes. After unzipping in Files, select the documents and use the share icon to attach them to a new message as ordinary files.
